## Facts and sources (checked, `00_STRATEGIE/sources/banque-2.md`, idea 20)

- Line 5: "Psychologists call it the hot-cold empathy gap" is the safe wording of L1 (Loewenstein, G. (2005), Hot-cold
  empathy gaps and medical decision making, *Health Psychology* 24(4 Suppl.), S49-S56). The card cites author, journal
  and year, like the carousel's slide 6.
- Line 7: the reading by analogy given in the bank ("In the DM they felt it. The next day, they don't feel it the same
  way.", level "en partie"). No study measured DM leads: it is a lens, not a result, and the caption's "The catch" says
  so. No duration is given (nothing says how fast a lead cools down); "tomorrow" is the bank's "the next day".
- Line 9: the safe wording of L5 (Read, D. & van Leeuwen, B. (1998), Predicting hunger, *OBHDP* 76(2), 189-205): "In
  one study, hungry people picked more junk food for next week than people who had just eaten." The coach's to-go burger
  is the joke, not a claim of the study.
- Lines 4 and 5: not the only cause. The coach's "she forgot" (rd84) gets a "Maybe." before the gap, so the gap is never
  the only explanation.
- Line 13: one question at a time (P6), two or three open times in the lead's time zone, booked on Cal.com, Calendly or
  iClosed (P9); on Calendly Free or another tool it sends the booking link (P9b): the caption's "The catch" says it.
  Never "instantly" (P4): "right in the chat" is about where it happens, not about speed.
- Not used, on purpose: the HBR study of 2011 (it sells speed); "book later" (no verbatim says it); rd97 (free trial
  sessions, not a DM).
- Line 2 and its card: the conversation is the carousel's example (slides 2 and 3), not a client's. rd47 is a coach's
  words ("even said 'this is exactly what I need'… and then vanished").
- Nothing invented: no number of calls, no client, no result. The lead in the chat has a generic name.
