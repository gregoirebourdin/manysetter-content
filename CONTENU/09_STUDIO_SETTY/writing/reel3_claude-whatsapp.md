## Facts and sources (checked)

- The tutorial (line 2): the open-source project whatsapp-mcp connects to your personal WhatsApp through WhatsApp Web's
  multi-device API (not the official API), with a QR code at the first launch and a block to add in
  `claude_desktop_config.json`; Claude uses its tools when you ask it. Source: https://github.com/lharries/whatsapp-mcp
  (README read on 19/10/2026).
- Lines 5 and 7: Claude Desktop is a chat app; it acts through the WhatsApp tools when you write to it. It does not
  answer your leads on its own while the laptop is closed.
- Line 9: WhatsApp's Terms list "bulk messaging, auto-messaging, auto-dialing" among impermissible uses and say "we may
  take action with respect to your account, including disabling or suspending your account". Source:
  https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/terms-of-service (read on 19/10/2026). Setty says "they can", never "they will".
- Line 11: the README warns that the server "could lead to private data exfiltration"; Invariant Labs showed on 7 April
  2025 that a malicious MCP server next to it can send your message history to another number. Source:
  https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/whatsapp-mcp-exploited
- Line 13: Manychat connects WhatsApp through Meta's Cloud API (the official WhatsApp Business Platform). Source: the
  site's guide `content/seo/guides-manychat-whatsapp-automation.mdx`. No code: brief § 2. Nothing promises to avoid a ban
  (verbatims rule 5).
- Line 15: replies on its own once live, in the brand's voice, hands off when the answer is not in the sources: brief § 2.
- Claude is never the villain: the hack is. The caption keeps "Not affiliated with Anthropic, WhatsApp or Meta."
